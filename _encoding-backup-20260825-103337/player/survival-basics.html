Survival Guide

Survival Basics

How to stay alive when the frontier stops being routine: Infected outbreaks, dangerous wildlife, bad positioning, low health, limited ammunition and failed escape plans.

Surviving an Infected Outbreak

Participating in a Western Dead outbreak does not mean standing in the middle of the street until one side is gone. Positioning, retreat routes and knowing when to disengage are often more important than raw kill count.

Do Not Get Surrounded

Open ground becomes dangerous once infected begin approaching from several directions. Keep space behind you and avoid committing to a position with no clear exit.

Fight Near an Escape Route

Pay attention to stairs, ladders, alleys, raised walkways and open roads you can use if the fight turns against you. A good position includes a way out.

Health Matters More Than Kills

If your health is getting low, stop treating the outbreak like a shooting contest. Break contact, recover if possible and return only when the situation is manageable.

Watch Your Ammunition

Long engagements can burn through ammunition quickly. Do not wait until the fight is collapsing to discover your best weapon is nearly empty.

Use High Ground When You Are Hurt

One of the strongest emergency survival tactics during an outbreak is getting above the infected. Reachable rooftops, porch roofs, platforms and other elevated positions can reduce the immediate pressure from enemies below.

Create Breathing Room

If your health is low, height can buy valuable time to reload, recover, assess the street below or decide whether the fight is worth continuing.

Do Not Stay Trapped

High ground is useful only if you still have a plan for leaving it. Avoid climbing somewhere that becomes a dead end once the surrounding area fills up.

Using the environment this way can feel like a major advantage, but learning where you can safely recover is part of surviving Western Dead.

Your Horse Is Not a Guaranteed Escape

A horse is usually the fastest way out of danger, but relying on it as your only survival plan is risky. Horses react to threats and can panic when surrounded by hostile activity.

Expect Panic Around Danger

Infected should be treated much like other severe threats around your mount. A frightened horse may become difficult to control, and severe panic may result in the rider being bucked off.

Have Another Way Out

If your horse becomes unusable during an outbreak, know where you can run, climb or take cover. Do not ride deep into a dense infected area without considering how you would escape on foot.

Fight Smart, Not Constantly

Distance Is Protection

Whenever possible, keep infected outside immediate melee range. Giving yourself room makes aiming, reloading and retreating much easier.

Do Not Chase Every Target

Running after one infected can pull you into a worse position or into another group. Let targets come into favorable ground instead of giving up your position unnecessarily.

Loot Only When It Is Safe

A corpse is not worth losing your character over. Clear the immediate threat before stopping to loot, especially when visibility is poor or more infected may still be nearby.

Leaving Is a Valid Choice

If you are badly hurt, undersupplied or overwhelmed, retreat. Surviving an outbreak is more valuable than forcing a fight you are no longer equipped to win.

Frontier Tricks Worth Knowing

Some useful mechanics are easy to overlook because the frontier does not always explain them directly.

Begging Can Help Dead Eye

Begging is not only something to do when money is tight. It can also help build Dead Eye over time, making it useful for a character who is still developing.

Crime Is About Witnesses

Violence outside populated towns is much less likely to create immediate law trouble when nobody is around to witness it. In settlements, being seen can turn a quiet crime into a much larger problem.

Loot Where You Cannot Be Seen

If you intend to loot a body in or near a populated area, pay attention to who can see you. Moving out of sight before looting can reduce the chance of attracting unwanted attention.

The Wilderness Gives You More Freedom

Remote areas generally give you more room to hunt, fight and loot without the same level of immediate attention found in towns. That freedom comes with risks from predators, distance and lack of nearby services.

Wilderness Survival

Outbreaks are not the only danger on the frontier. Distance from town can turn ordinary mistakes into serious problems.

Predators Change a Hunt Fast

Mountain lions, wolves, coyotes and bears can interrupt hunting or travel without much warning. Keep enough awareness and ammunition to deal with something other than your intended prey.

Know Where Civilization Is

Before traveling deep into the wilderness, know the nearest settlement, Wayfarer's Post or reliable route back. Getting lost while low on supplies makes every other problem worse.

Do Not Let Supplies Reach Zero

Food, water and ammunition are easiest to replace before they become emergencies. Turn back while you still have options rather than after the problem has already started.

Pick Your Battles

Not every predator, outlaw or infected group needs to be fought. Avoiding unnecessary danger is one of the simplest ways to survive longer.

Quick Outbreak Checklist

Before Entering

Check health, ammunition, food, water and your escape route. If one of those is already a problem, solve it before joining the fight.

When Things Go Bad

Create distance, move toward high ground or an exit, stop looting, protect your remaining health and do not assume your horse will always save you.
