Character Record

Statistics

What your character does on the frontier can become part of their permanent record. Hunting, prospecting, surviving the infected, committing robberies and other supported actions can build lifetime statistics over time.

Persistent Character History

Actions Become Statistics

RDR2PF tracks selected gameplay actions for each individual character. When a supported action is completed, the resource responsible for that activity can report the result to the character progression system.

The statistic is then added to that character's saved lifetime record. These totals are not meant to represent a temporary session score. They are a history of what that character has actually done across their life on the server.

Not every animation, button press or ordinary action becomes a statistic. A gameplay system must specifically report a supported event before it is counted.

Fishing

Fishing Records

The statistics system is capable of recording fish caught, total fish weight, largest fish and fish sold.

These values can describe both how often a character fishes and, where supported by the fishing system, the quality or scale of their catches.

Hunting

Hunting Records

Supported hunting statistics include animals killed, animals skinned, legendary animals killed, pelts sold and meat harvested.

Animal kills are already detected by the character progression resource and recorded when the game confirms the player's kill.

Cooking

Cooking Records

The system can record meals cooked, stews cooked, coffee brewed and tonics crafted as the corresponding gameplay systems are connected to character progression.

Mining

Mining Records

Supported mining statistics include rocks mined, ore mined, coal mined, iron mined, gold found and gems found.

These counters allow mining to be measured by actual completed work rather than simply by time spent standing at a mining location.

Lumber

Lumberjack Records

The statistics system can record trees chopped, logs cut and firewood produced.

Gold Country

Gold Prospecting

Successful prospecting can increase the character's Gold Prospected statistic.

This is connected directly to the RDR2PF Gold Prospecting system, so successful prospecting results can become part of the character's permanent record.

Crime

Outlaw Records

The system supports outlaw actions, ambushes survived, robberies, thefts, items stolen, money stolen and law warnings.

Successful robbery activity is already connected to character progression. A criminal reputation therefore can emerge from repeated actions rather than from simply selecting an outlaw job.

Combat

Combat & Survival

Supported combat statistics include infected killed, humans killed, headshots, melee kills and deaths.

Both the persistent zombie system and Western Dead outbreak systems can report infected kills to character progression.

Character Identity

How Your Title Is Calculated

A character who has no qualifying profession history begins as a Drifter.

For title calculation, RDR2PF compares specific lifetime statistics associated with different frontier paths. The profession with the greatest qualifying total becomes the character's displayed title.

This means a title is intended to emerge from the way the character is actually played. A character who spends much of their life hunting may naturally become known as a Hunter, while someone who spends far more time prospecting may eventually be known as a Gold Miner.

Separate Progression

Profession XP & Levels

Lifetime statistics and profession levels are related to character activity, but they are separate systems.

Statistics record what the character has done. Profession XP is used for level progression and can eventually be used by gameplay systems to require experience before certain actions, recipes, equipment or opportunities become available.

Profession progression begins at Level 1 and currently has a maximum of Level 50.

Progress XP Required
Level 1 → 2100 XP
Level 2 → 3150 XP
Level 3 → 4200 XP
Level 4 → 5250 XP
Each later levelPrevious requirement + 50 XP

The formula is simple: the first level requires 100 XP, and the requirement increases by 50 XP for every level after that.

Time Investment

How Long Does Progress Take?

There is deliberately no universal conversion such as “one hour equals one level.” Progress is based on completed gameplay actions, and different activities happen at very different speeds.

Catching a fish, locating and killing an animal, completing a prospecting cycle, finding usable ore or completing a successful robbery can each require different amounts of travel, preparation, risk and luck.

The amount of real time required therefore depends on several things: the profession being pursued, how frequently qualifying actions can be completed, the XP awarded by that activity, travel time, available resources, player skill and the conditions encountered in the world.

Early levels should naturally move faster because they require less XP. Later levels become increasingly long-term goals. The system is intended to reward repeated play over time rather than encourage a character to reach maximum progression in a single short session.

What Counts

Implemented vs. Supported Statistics

The character progression resource contains a larger registry of statistics that RDR2PF can accept. A statistic appearing in that registry does not automatically mean every gameplay resource is already reporting it.

Some statistics are already wired directly into active systems, including animal kills, infected kills, successful robberies and Gold Prospecting. Other supported counters will begin accumulating as their corresponding profession systems are connected or expanded.

This lets the statistics system grow without replacing a character's existing record each time another profession gains progression support.

Remember

Your Record Belongs to the Character

Statistics and progression follow the individual character. They are designed to become a history of that person's life in RDR2PF: Western Dead — what they hunted, what they survived, what they worked at and what kind of reputation they built along the way.
